# Nail Your Raise: Startup Pitch Checklist
*Based on the presentation points from "Nail Your Raise: Luring VCs" by Vinod Khosla.*

Use this document to evaluate your startup narrative, slide deck structure, and investor psychology before you step into a pitch.

## 1. Core Axioms & Investor Psychology
*Investors are judging you in every way. They want to see if you are a clear thinker, if you get straight to the point, and if you are confident yet humble.*
- [ ] **Appeal to Emotion:** Does your pitch rely on a strong narrative? (Remember: 90% of an investor's decision is based on emotions and narratives. Investors bounce between fear and greed).
- [ ] **Simplicity:** Is your pitch completely simple and jargon-free? 
- [ ] **Transparency:** Are you hiding anything? (Play games or hide things, and good investors will notice. Be brutally honest about what you know and don't know).

## 2. Preparation & Stress-Testing
*To do the prep correctly, there must be a culture of speaking up and pushback.*
- [ ] **Air the Doubts:** Have you and your team externalized all anxieties, sticking points, uncertainties, and dreads?
- [ ] **Team Pushback:** Have you rigorously stress-tested the presentation internally before taking it to investors?

## 3. The Narrative Arc & Hooks
*Don't subvert your story in service of logical order. The narrative arc reigns supreme.*
- [ ] **The 30-Second Teaser:** Do you have a compelling 30-second "commercial teaser" that hooks the audience immediately?
- [ ] **The 120-Second Rule:** Within the first 60 to 120 seconds, do investors know exactly what you do and why it can change the world? (If an investor doesn’t know what your company does 5 minutes in, you're sunk).
- [ ] **Pacing the Complexity:** Do you start with the basics (as if speaking to kindergartners) but quickly advance your arguments (as if speaking to graduate students)?
- [ ] **Framing Risk:** Do you frame risks as manageable while emphasizing the massive market? (Use frameworks like: *"If I can prove X, then [massive outcome Y] happens"*).

## 4. Slide Structure (The Headline Test)
*Budgeting and data are for completeness, but they do not trump the emotional narrative.*
- [ ] **The Text Document Test:** Did you write out your ~20 slide headlines in a blank text document first? Does reading *just* the headlines tell a complete, emotional investment story?
- [ ] **Messages > Titles:** Is every headline a definitive statement rather than a generic category? (e.g., Use *"Team engineered for this specific opportunity"* instead of just *"Team"*).
- [ ] **The Rule of One:** Does each individual slide convey exactly **one** message? 

## 5. Visuals & Slide Design
*Less is more. Viewers' eyes should know exactly where to focus without wandering.*
- [ ] **Kill the Clutter:** Have you removed all gratuitous graphics, extra text, and visual distractions? Do not push text all the way to the edges of the slide.
- [ ] **Graphics as Support:** Are images only used if they convey a crystal-clear message, product picture, or visual aid? (Avoid using graphics as a crutch).
- [ ] **Show, Don't Tell:** Have you eliminated empty superlatives? Are you providing tangible proof instead of just claiming to be "the best"?
- [ ] **Wayfinding:** Do you start with an agenda/hook and visually remind the audience where they are in the story as you transition?

## 6. Essential Content Areas (Recommended ~15-20 Slides Total)
- [ ] **Tactics (2-3 Slides):** Have you clearly defined your go-to-market strategy, market segmentation, and proprietary business model?
- [ ] **Team Uniqueness (1 Slide):** Have you highlighted the team's unique advantages *and* honestly acknowledged your "additional needs" (future hires)?
- [ ] **Financials & Contingencies (3 Slides):** Have you included clear projections for revenue, cash flows, and contingencies?
