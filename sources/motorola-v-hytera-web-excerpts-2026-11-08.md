## 7th Circuit opinion, 108 F.4th 458 (7th Cir. July 2, 2024), Nos. 22-2370, 22-2413
url: https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-7th-circuit/116339567.html

- June 2007: Hytera CEO Chen Qingzhou contacted Motorola Malaysia engineer G.S. Kok, saying Hytera
  hoped to set up an R&D centre in Malaysia. Kok was offered "600,000 shares of Hytera stock" (about
  $2.5M when Hytera listed). Kok helped hire Y.T. Kok and Sam Chia; Y.T. Kok stayed at Motorola while
  secretly on Hytera's payroll.
- June 2008: Y.T. Kok downloaded over 100 documents at Hytera's specific request. Y.T. Kok and Chia
  downloaded "more than 10,000 technical documents" from Motorola's ClearCase and COMPASS databases,
  including DMR source code. Motorola argued more than 1,600 were still at Hytera at trial.
- Stolen code segments were inserted into Hytera products; identical minor coding errors appeared in
  both; Hytera modified code to conceal origin; documents circulated with Motorola logo replaced by
  Hytera's, sometimes not.
- 2010-2014: Hytera sold professional-tier DMR radios "functionally indistinguishable" from Motorola's,
  worldwide including the US, and marketed them at US trade shows.
- Source code described as "perhaps the most valuable part of a functional DMR radio"; Hytera had
  struggled to develop its own DMR code before the theft.
- Suit filed March 2017 (DTSA, Illinois TSA); copyright claims added August 2018; trial November 2019,
  about three and a half months.
- Verdict $764.6M = $345.8M compensatory + $418.8M punitive. Final judgment $543.7M.
- Copyright unjust enrichment (2010 to May 2016): $136.3M, reversed in part: no foreign sales because
  Motorola did not prove a domestic predicate act (download likely from server in Malaysia); remand to
  recalculate and apportion; likely reduced "perhaps by roughly an order of magnitude".
- DTSA unjust enrichment (May 2016 to June 2019): $135.8M (jury's $209.4M less $73.6M avoided R&D,
  double counting). DTSA punitive $271.6M (2x). Both affirmed. DTSA reaches worldwide sales because US
  trade-show marketing was a domestic act in furtherance. First such holding in any circuit.
- Copyright limitations: discovery rule applies (Warner Chappell v. Nealy).
- Dec 2020: permanent injunction denied; ongoing royalty set at "100 percent of Hytera's profits" on
  infringing products from July 2019. Remand to reconsider injunction (Rule 60(b)) given non-payment,
  earlier contempt, and the "long-secret Shenzhen case".

## District court post-trial opinion (N.D. Ill. 2020)
url: https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-dis-crt-n-d-ill-eas-div/2095416.html

- Hytera argued Motorola "had numerous reasons to suspect before March 2012" and could have confirmed
  "before March 2014 by pulling Compass logs". Motorola showed an internal investigation that concluded
  no issue existed. Jury found Hytera concealed the theft.
- June 2008 G.S. Kok email: they "needed to re-write softwares to look different from Motorola" to
  "protect the company from impending lawsuits".
- Identical misspellings in both codebases (PTX-2090, PTX-2091) cited as strong evidence; Motorola
  identified the copied code in each version of its DMR software. Expert: Dr. Wicker.
- Evidence deleted by Huang and Chia; more than 100 Fifth Amendment invocations by former Motorola
  employees, adverse-inference instruction upheld.

## Motorola Solutions, history of case
url: https://www.motorolasolutions.com/en_us/about/us-district-court-victory/history-of-case.html

- March 14, 2017 suit; during pre-trial discovery Motorola found Hytera copied its source code into
  Hytera products; July 30, 2018 copyright claims added. Alleges "more than 10,000" documents and
  "millions of lines" of source code taken, with steps to conceal.

## DOJ criminal case (trade press)
urls: https://www.theregister.com/2022/02/08/hytera_motorola_ip_theft_accusation/ ;
https://urgentcomm.com/litigation/hytera-pleads-guilty-to-criminal-conspiracy-must-make-full-restitution-to-motorola-solutions ;

https://urgentcomm.com/litigation/hytera-owes-more-than-200m-may-get-criminal-sentence-on-march-5

- February 2022: 21-count indictment (N.D. Ill.), filed under seal about nine months earlier. Alleges
  recruitment of Motorola Malaysia employees 2007-2020 with pay rises in exchange for proprietary
  information; trade secrets included hardware, radio software architecture, DMR source code.
- Criminal complaint (2022): Hytera DMR code "contained hundreds of thousands of lines of copied code";
  copying included code, comments, "even typos". Seven individuals charged.
- G.S. (Gee Sion) Kok pleaded guilty (2022 or 2023 per sources); six others at large.
- January 13, 2025: Hytera pleaded guilty to one count of conspiracy to steal trade secrets; fine up to
  $60M plus full restitution. Government sought more than $350M; Hytera proposed a fine of
  $98,000-196,000 and no restitution.
- March 2026: Judge Tharp fined Hytera $50M and ordered $214M restitution, offset by civil payments.

## Collection, royalties, contempt

https://rallies.ai/news/motorola-solutions-receives-73-million-from-hytera-in-trade-secrets-case-in-first-nine-months-of-2026-c221c0423dd65a4e

- Original judgment with interest, fees, costs: $631.6M ($543.7M + $51.1M interest + $2.6M costs +
  $34.2M fees). Hytera's two US subsidiaries entered bankruptcy in 2020.
- Paid: $232M by mid-2026 ($60M in H1 2026); $245M by October 3, 2026 ($73M in the first nine months
  of 2026). Motorola recognised gains about $15M (2022), $61M (2024), $157M (2025), $60M (H1 2026).
- August 2023: contempt over unpaid royalties; Hytera paid $56M into escrow. I-Series royalties $61M
  received in 2024.
- June 2022: Hytera secretly filed a Shenzhen declaratory suit that H-Series did not infringe; Motorola
  served November 2023. March 25, 2024 anti-suit injunction; April 2, 2024 contempt sanctions: worldwide
  sales suspension, $1M per day, notice to customers. Shenzhen suit withdrawn April 7, 2024. 7th Circuit
  stayed the sales ban and fines.
- August 2025 (Judge Pacold): H-Series held substantially like the royalty-bearing products; Hytera in
  contempt; over $59M royalties plus over $11M interest through Q1 2024; Hytera reports about $116M
  H-Series royalties owed; under appeal. Third contempt since 2023 per Motorola.
- Copyright remand: January 2025 Motorola asked for $14.6M (down from $136.3M); Hytera says $0; ruling
  pending as of late 2026.
- Supreme Court denied Hytera's petition (No. 24-725), February 2025.

## Australia: Hytera v Motorola [2024] FCAFC 168 (Full Court, 18 December 2024)
url: https://sprintlaw.com.au/business-law-library/cases/fcafc/2024/2024-fcafc-168/

- Motorola began DMR work in 2003; Hytera's project in disarray late 2007; Hytera DMR to market March
  2010; Australia from September 2013. July 2017 patent suit; December 2018 copyright claims added after
  Hytera's source code surfaced in the patent case. Copyright in 11 programs; infringement via importing
  devices with firmware compiled from Hytera source and offering firmware downloads.
- Hytera did not dispute that G.S. Kok and 12 others took Motorola code and used some in firmware.
  Primary judge: all Motorola DMR source code used as a resource, "industrial scale harvesting";
  Hytera deliberately deleted source code. Hytera reached market at least 15 months earlier than
  otherwise. Additional damages for copyright; further hearing ordered on substantial part.

## England: Motorola v Hytera [2025] EWCA Civ 1667 (19 December 2025)
url: https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Motorola-v-Hytera.pdf

- Protection of Trading Interests Act 1980 s.5 bars enforcing a "judgment for multiple damages"; Court
  of Appeal held it bars the entire DTSA award, compensatory element included (first appeal dismissed).
- Copyright element: English judgment for $136.3M (November 2023), stayed on $25M paid into court;
  revocation after the 7th Circuit reversal set aside, stay continued pending US reassessment.
