Status checked 2026-11-08.

| Step | Date | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jury verdict | Feb 2020 | $764.6M ($345.8M compensatory + $418.8M punitive) |
| Judgment (court set the equitable awards) | Dec 2020 | $543.7M; $631.6M with $51.1M interest, $34.2M fees, $2.6M costs |
| 7th Circuit | Jul 2024 | DTSA $135.8M compensatory + $271.6M punitive (2x) = $407.4M affirmed; $136.3M copyright award reversed in part and remanded |
| Supreme Court | Feb 2025 | Hytera's petition denied |
| Copyright remand | pending | Motorola asks $14.6M (was $136.3M); Hytera says $0 |
| Ongoing royalty | from Jul 2019 | 100% of Hytera's profits on infringing radios; I-Series $61M received 2024; H-Series about $116M owed per Hytera, under appeal |
| Criminal case | plea Jan 2025, sentence Mar 2026 | $50M fine + $214M restitution, offset by civil payments |
| Collected | to Oct 3, 2026 | about $245M |

Lessons from the numbers:
- The DTSA reached Hytera's worldwide sales because Hytera marketed the radios at US trade shows. Copyright
  could not, because the download happened in Malaysia. That is why the copyright award fell about 90%.
- Avoided R&D ($73.6M) was part of the award: the court priced the stolen code by what Hytera did not
  have to spend. The Australian court found Hytera reached market at least 15 months earlier.
- Collection is slow. See [[collecting-from-hytera-took-contempt-orders-in-several-countries]].
