Thales announced Sentinel Envelope Plus on 1 October 2026 as an available paid add-on targeting AI-assisted analysis of distributed applications. Selected binary functions are lifted, transformed and recompiled without customer source changes. This is direct competitive evidence against a claim that established protectors ignore AI; availability is not disclosed sales or adoption.

Its accompanying experiment used a 161 KB application with ten deliberately seeded vulnerabilities and Claude Opus 4.7. Thales reports eight findings in an unprotected run lasting 3m10s, versus none in a protected session lasting 6h43m. Additional dynamic-analysis tooling was reportedly provided when requested. The vendor reports 342,000 versus 332 million tokens, but does not make these numbers a general independently replicated result.

The task was vulnerability discovery, not algorithm reconstruction. A selected application, vendor-run evaluation and reported stopping point cannot establish universal resistance, typical attacker cost, customer losses or demand for formal proofs. Defects remained present.
