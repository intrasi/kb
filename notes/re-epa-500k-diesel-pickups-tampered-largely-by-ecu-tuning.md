EPA's own page (updated 15 September 2025) says controls on over 500,000 diesel pickups, about 13% of
those originally certified with controls, were removed, and that the excess NOx equals adding 9 million
trucks. The underlying November 2020 Air Enforcement Division report gives more than 550,000 trucks
and more than 570,000 tons of NOx (search summaries; PDF text not extracted).

EPA defines tuning as "replacing or altering the software or calibrations that control engine
operation". Tuners must locate the right logic and tables in the engine controller's firmware first,
which is reverse engineering of a shipped artifact. Enforcement is large: EPA lists dozens of cases,
and in the Punch It Performance case the defendants surrendered their programming files.

Moderate confidence: the share that is software-only tuning versus hardware deletes is not split out
here. Not every case is AI-assisted; AI lowering the cost of finding calibrations is our argument
([[re-agents-route-around-obfuscation]]), not EPA's.

Why it matters: a measured, regulator-sourced harm from reverse engineering device firmware, at scale.
It also answers the right-to-repair objection: this tampering is illegal, repair access is not.
