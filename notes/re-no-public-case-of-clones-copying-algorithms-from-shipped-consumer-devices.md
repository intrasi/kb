Searched English-language court, DOJ and press records (2015-2026):
- Insider theft carries the big money: Motorola v. Hytera ($407.4M affirmed 2024), ASML v. XTAL ($845M,
  uncollected), Samsung leaks to Chinese firms (CXMT case, about 5 trillion won estimated loss; 10
  indicted December 2025), Sinovel (over $550M). Korea's intelligence service counted 107 overseas
  technology leaks in 2021-2025, mostly chips and displays. Protecting shipped code stops none of these.
- Consumer-device rivalries are patent and design cases (iRobot/SharkNinja, Dyson vs Dreame and Laifen
  in China, DJI/Autel, Ecovacs/Roborock), often lost by the plaintiff when patents are invalidated.
- Copying of the shipped product is documented for consumables and accessories (clone cartridges,
  chargers) and in one firmware case, Ubiquiti v. Cambium (settled 2020).

Why it matters: do not pitch "Chinese clones steal your algorithm" as a documented loss. Use it only as
a fear and a vision line ("your patents keep failing, so protect the know-how in the binary"), and say
insider theft needs other controls. Chinese court records were not searched; that is the cheapest gap
to close. Related: [[re-big-machine-software-theft-cases-started-with-insiders]]. Checked 2026-11-08.
