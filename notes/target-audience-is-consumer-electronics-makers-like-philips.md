## Who we sell to

**Consumer electronics companies whose "smart" feature is an algorithm running inside the device.**
A cheaper competitor can buy the product, pull out the firmware, and use AI to copy that algorithm.

## Example companies

| Company | Products where the algorithm matters |
| --- | --- |
| Philips | Sonicare toothbrushes, shavers, personal care |
| Dyson | Hair dryers, vacuums, air purifiers |
| Samsung, LG | Home appliances, TVs, robot vacuums |
| Sony, Bose, Sennheiser, Bang & Olufsen | Headphones (noise cancelling), speakers |
| Oral-B / Braun | Smart toothbrushes, shavers |
| Garmin, Polar, Suunto, Oura | Watches and rings (sleep, fitness scores) |
| iRobot, Husqvarna | Robot vacuums, robot mowers |
| Miele, Bosch home appliances, Electrolux, De'Longhi | Smart washing machines, ovens, coffee machines |

## Why they would buy

- They spend years building the smart feature; a competitor copying it skips that work.
- Patents often fail to protect it (Dyson's hair-dryer patents were invalidated in China,
  [[re-no-public-case-of-clones-copying-algorithms-from-shipped-consumer-devices]]).
- AI makes reading firmware fast and cheap.

## Who to talk to inside

The head of product security, or the head of firmware / embedded software.

## Next step

Talk to 5-10 of these companies and ask: which algorithm in your device would hurt most if a
competitor copied it, and what do you do today to protect it?
