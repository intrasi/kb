Software Directive 2009/24/EC: Article 5(3) (observe, study, test) and Article 6 (decompile for
interoperability) cannot be overridden by contract (Article 8). The Court of Justice (Top System,
C-13/20, 2021) added decompiling to correct errors. The Trade Secrets Directive 2016/943, Article
3(1)(b), treats taking apart a lawfully obtained product as lawful acquisition unless a valid duty says
otherwise. What makers keep: Article 6(2) forbids using decompiled information to build a substantially
similar program, and Article 7(1)(c) gives remedies against circumvention tools. Obfuscation can count
toward the "reasonable steps" needed to keep trade-secret status (secondary).

US: DMCA §1201 repair exemptions for vehicles and farm machines (since 2015, widened each cycle) do not
excuse EPA or DOT violations; FTC v. Deere (July 2026) requires dealer-equal repair tools.

Why it matters: we can sell protection against cloning and illegal tampering, never against owners and
repairers. Pitch line: "protect the code, not the machine from its owner". Not legal advice; read the
directive articles before quoting. Checked 2026-11-08.
