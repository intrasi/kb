- Hytera's two US subsidiaries entered bankruptcy in 2020. Hytera did not pay the royalty, and was held in
  contempt in August 2023 (paid $56M into escrow), April 2024 and August 2025. Motorola calls it three
  contempts since 2023.
- June 2022: Hytera secretly filed a suit in Shenzhen asking for a ruling that its redesigned H-Series did
  not use Motorola's secrets. Motorola was served 17 months later. A US anti-suit injunction (March 2024)
  and sanctions (worldwide sales ban, $1M per day) forced withdrawal; the 7th Circuit stayed the
  sanctions. August 2025: the H-Series was held to be substantially the same and royalty-bearing.
- England (Court of Appeal, December 2025): the Protection of Trading Interests Act 1980 blocks enforcing
  any part of the DTSA award, because it includes doubled damages. Only the copyright judgment can be
  enforced there, stayed until the US reassessment.
- Separate German patent cases (2018) won injunctions and a recall. The FCC also put Hytera on the
  Covered List (2021), barring new broadband-capable equipment, but not its narrowband radios.

Why it matters: even with liability conceded, nine years after suing Motorola had collected under half
the affirmed and royalty amounts. For a device maker, a court win against a foreign rival is slow and
partial. Prevention and fast proof are worth more than a verdict.
