The guidebook's six criteria, verbatim headings:

1. Team & Execution: how strong is the team and how fast can it execute?
2. Technical Complexity: did you build something real and technically solid?
3. Is It Something People Want: is there a clear user need or demand?
4. Originality: is the solution new or meaningfully different from existing approaches?
5. Ambition: could this work as a product on its own, beyond a weekend prototype?
6. Demo and presentation: show the core value fast; what, why, and how.

Why it matters to us: criterion 3 needs evidence from outside the team, so the public
listening in [[do-people-need-ai-resistant-code-protection]] feeds the score directly.
Criterion 4 is where incumbents bite: the deck must say plainly how the product differs
from vendors already marketing protection against AI attackers
([[code-protection-field-open-questions]]).
