# Tarpit patterns

Severity: H high, M moderate. Each row gives the structural cause and the question that
tests whether the idea escapes it.

| # | Pattern | Sev | Why it fails | Test question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Parking, hyperlocal logistics | H | Landlords own the supply, cities regulate it, and users pay almost nothing to save minutes | Who owns the inventory, and have they agreed to give it to you? |
| 2 | Generic todo or notes app | H | Incumbents have years of polish; switching needs a 10x reason | What makes a Notion user switch and stay after 30 days? |
| 3 | AI wrapper with no moat | M | A prompt plus an API clones in a weekend | If a model lab ships this next month, what data, integration, or relationship still protects you? |
| 4 | Crypto X for Y | M | The chain solves the founder's question, and a database works better | What breaks if you use a normal database? |
| 5 | Uber for X | H | Supply and demand at once, with unit economics thinner than Uber's | Do per-transaction economics work, and who are your first 100 suppliers? |
| 6 | Social network for a niche | H | It has no value at 10 users, and Discord and Reddit host niches for free | What does it do at 10 users? Could it be a Discord bot? |
| 7 | Unsupplied marketplace | H | A marketplace with no locked supply is only a search bar | Can you be the supply for the first 100 transactions? |
| 8 | Browser extension | M | Nobody browses the extension store; the extension is a feature of someone else's product | How does anyone find it, and what is the path to payment? |
| 9 | Discover-new-things app | H | The options are finite, Google Maps absorbed the category, and users want to pick fast | Why would anyone open it on day 30? |
| 10 | Better clone of an incumbent | M | Your friends are not the market; switching costs are real | Do the incumbent's millions of users share your complaint? |
| 11 | Dating app | H | It needs liquidity in one place at one time, and success means churn | How do you get 1,000 users on each side in one city in one week? |
| 12 | Tool for students or creators | M | Low willingness to pay, churn at graduation, and free tools are good enough | What is the monthly price, and is there a B2B buyer (a university, a brand)? |
| 13 | Software sold to small firms one by one | M | Low revenue per firm, fast churn, and acquisition cost above lifetime value | Which channel (accountants, banks, a mandatory system) reaches 1,000 firms at once? |
| 14 | Dashboards for firms with no analyst | M | Owners want an action rather than a chart; nobody opens it after the first month | What does the product do, and who opens it on day 30? |

Rows 1 to 12: tarpit-mcp, after Dalton Caldwell and Michael Seibel (YC). Rows 13 and 14 were
added from general software-business economics (moderate confidence).

## Heuristics

- Consumer is the largest tarpit category.
- If many smart people failed, find what specifically killed them.
- Defensiveness when the risk is named is the tarpit talking.
- B2B in a vertical where a founder has unfair insight is the opposite of a tarpit.
- If YC already funded this exact idea, name what you add.
