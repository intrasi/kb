Distinguish RE's enabling role, access prerequisites, demonstrated outcomes, possible consequences, remediation and evidence of actual malicious use or AI assistance. Follow up [[reverse-engineering-targets-and-intelligence-map]] without equating all device insecurity with an obfuscation market.

- Robot vacuums, smart access controls, infusion pumps, railway passenger systems, EV charging, retail/building equipment and medical robots.

## Answer

- [[everyday-device-reverse-engineering-harm-casebook]] — detailed Serbian casebook: everyday objects, access conditions, observed effects, actual-abuse evidence and limits; train screens treated explicitly as a scenario rather than a proven brake-compromise chain.
- [[ai-assisted-consumer-robot-research-connects-reverse-engineering-to-physical-and-privacy-risks]] — March 2026 preprint on AI-assisted research into mower, exoskeleton and window cleaner; timing and validation caveats included.
- [[device-harm-evidence-does-not-establish-obfuscation-as-the-main-remedy]] — differentiates exposure, exploitability, obfuscation and the security/safety properties a product would need to establish.
- [[everyday-device-harm-primary-excerpts-2026-11-07]] — original research, vendor advisories, official publications and original news reporting.

The evidence supports concrete mechanisms and some observed malicious device misuse. It does not quantify an inevitable future attack wave, demonstrate AI use in historical cases, or validate willingness to pay for our proposed product. No devices were attacked or tested.
